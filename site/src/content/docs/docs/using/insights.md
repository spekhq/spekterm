**Conversation metrics** shows how you work with Claude Code: when you work, how long you stay, how you
talk to the agent, and what you have it do. It has two tabs:

- **Numbers**: charts counted from your conversations. Opening them costs nothing and sends nothing anywhere.
- **Reading**: a written reading of how you talk to your agent, generated by your own `claude` only when you
  ask for one, and kept with its date.

Open it with **Conversation metrics** on the activity bar (the column at the far left). It covers the whole
window. `Esc` or **✕ Esc** closes it, and focus returns to the activity bar. While it is open, spekterm's
other shortcuts are paused, as with any dialog.

## Where the numbers come from

Claude Code writes a record of every conversation under `~/.claude/projects` and, by default, deletes it after
30 days. spekterm reads those records and keeps a copy of the parts it counts in its own data folder, so the
numbers still cover months that Claude Code has already deleted.

- The records are read once after spekterm starts and again each time you open Conversation metrics. While
  that runs, the view says **Scanning transcripts…**.
- It covers **every project** Claude Code has a record for, not only the folders on your rail and not only
  the selected one. Sessions started outside spekterm count too.
- If you keep Claude Code's data somewhere else, set `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` in your shell and restart spekterm.
  When there is no record to read, the view says the source was not found rather than showing empty charts.
  A source that exists but has nothing in it yet says that instead.
- "Your messages" are the ones you typed. Text Claude Code or a tool added to the conversation on your behalf
  is left out, and **Records not counted as mine** says how much that was.
- The sessions spekterm itself runs to write a reading (below) are never counted.

## Numbers

Choose the time range at the top: **All time**, or the last 7, 30 or 90 days. Every chart follows it.

The charts come in four groups:

- **Rhythm**: **Rhythm of the week** (your messages per weekday and hour, in your time zone) and **How long I
  sit down for** (work stretches, split wherever nothing happened for a while).
- **Habits**: **How long I talk** (characters per message), **Turns per conversation**, and **When I hit the
  brakes** (how often you interrupted the agent, by day).
- **What I work on**: **Which project takes my words**, **What Bash actually runs** (the first word of every
  command, subagents included), and **Skills I actually use** (with subagent types).
- **How I talk**: **Literal cues** (messages that end with a question, open with a command word, contain a
  correction, a hedge or a polite word, or are only an acknowledgement), **Chinese and English**, and **The
  things I say most**.

Distributions show the median and the 75th and 90th percentiles, never the average: a few very long sessions
would pull an average far from what is typical. Each chart names, under **source**, which part of the record
it was counted from. **Literal cues** shows its rule and a few real messages for each cue, so you can judge
whether it counts what you think it counts. It matches words, not tone.

Your own words appear in only two places: short messages you sent at least three times (**The things I say
most**), and short examples under **Literal cues**. There is no view that lists your messages.

## Reading

**Generate a reading** asks your own `claude` to read the messages you typed in the range selected on the
Numbers tab, and to write what it notices about how you talk to your agent. It never runs on its own: not at
startup, not when you open the tab.

Before anything is sent, spekterm asks **Send these messages to Anthropic?** and shows:

- the period, the number of projects and messages, and how many characters will be sent;
- the model requested;
- that only the text you typed is sent: no tool output, no file contents from your repositories.

If the range holds more than one run can carry, the oldest part is dropped first, and the numbers shown are
those of what is actually sent. Nothing leaves your computer until you choose **Send and generate**.

A reading takes a few minutes and has a cost, which it records.

- **It runs as your `claude` login.** Credentials that live only in your shell environment, such as an API
  key, are not passed on. If that is the only way you sign in, the reading fails, and the message says so.
- It runs in an empty folder of spekterm's, with no tools, so it reads none of your repositories. The
  conversation Claude Code records for it is deleted when it ends.
- **Every observation carries a quote** from your messages. spekterm checks each quote against what you
  actually typed and drops any observation whose quote it cannot find. The reading says how many were
  dropped. If none survives, nothing is saved.

Readings are kept side by side in spekterm's data folder, readable only by your user account, and listed
under **Saved readings**, newest first. A new reading never replaces an older one, so you can compare what
it said months apart. Each reading shows when it was generated, the period it covers, the model requested,
how much it read, how many observations were dropped, its cost, and whether its own conversation was
deleted. When its period is not the range selected on the Numbers tab, it says so.

See [Data and network](/docs/reference/data-and-network/) for what is stored and what is sent.
